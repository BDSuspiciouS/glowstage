Glowstage 0.9.9: rev lights for Forza, F1, iRacing and Assetto Corsa in your room, WiZ lights, Razer and SteelSeries devices through Synapse and GG, a someone's-home mode for your holidays, your phone remote as an app on your home screen, Ctrl+K that understands "desk red" or "30%", a faster start, and a window that works well with a keyboard and a screen reader.

**Install:** download `Glowstage-0.9.9-windows.zip`, unzip it anywhere and run `Glowstage.exe`. It adds itself to the Start menu. Windows may say the publisher is unknown: choose *More info → Run anyway*. **Updating?** From 0.9.4, click *Update now* on Home. From an older version, quit Glowstage from its tray icon and unzip over the old folder: your lights and settings stay.

**New**
- Rev lights for racing games (Setup → Games → *Racing games*): Forza (Motorsport and Horizon) and EA Sports F1 23 and F1 24 can send their telemetry to your PC, an option in the game's own settings. Your lights then fill up like a rev counter from left to right, green, yellow and red, and pulse blue at the limiter (never more than three times a second). In F1 the five red start lights come on across your setup and go out together at lights out, a yellow flag or the safety car shows as a slow amber band, your fastest lap as a purple sweep, and the chequered flag as a slow black and white scroll. Slow lights like Hue show a calm green to red instead. Nothing reads the game: it sends, Glowstage listens on this PC only, and only while the switch is on.
- iRacing, Assetto Corsa and Assetto Corsa Competizione too, with nothing to set up: they publish their telemetry for dashboards and wheels on your PC, and Glowstage reads it while you drive. Your lights follow the revs (with the car's own shift points in iRacing), and a yellow flag or a caution shows as the amber band.
- Razer devices through Razer Synapse *(beta)* (Setup → *Razer devices*): when Synapse runs it keeps your Razer keyboard, mouse, headset or mouse mat from OpenRGB. Switch this on and they become lights in Glowstage anyway, through Razer's own Chroma SDK: colors, presets, effects and the light show. Synapse gets them back when you switch it off or quit.
- SteelSeries devices through SteelSeries GG *(beta)* (Setup → *SteelSeries devices*): the same for SteelSeries keyboards (per key), mice and headsets while GG runs, through SteelSeries' own GameSense. GG gets them back when you switch it off or quit.
- WiZ lights *(beta)*: Philips WiZ and other WiZ bulbs, strips and lamps are found by themselves on your network once *Allow local communication* is on in the WiZ app. Colors, whites by color temperature, brightness, their own 32 scenes, and every Glowstage look and the light show.
- Someone's home, while you're away (Setup → Schedule → *While you're away*): with the PC left on, your room's lights (or the groups you pick) come on around sunset or a time you choose and go off around bedtime, a little earlier or later each day, and now and then one light goes off and another comes on during the evening, so from outside it looks like someone is home. Plain warm white, no effects. Schedules and automatic looks keep their hands off meanwhile, your PC's lights stay as they are, and *Stop* on Home puts everything back as it was.
- Ctrl+K understands what you type: "red", "desk blue", "30%", "2700k", "pc off", "storm", "focus 50 10", "sleep 45", "pause 1h" or "resume", and Enter does it (with Undo where it changes your lights). A light or group with that name gets it alone; with nothing named, the lights that are on do. A preset's exact name still goes first, so a preset called *Red room* stays the preset.
- Your phone remote as an app: add the page to your phone's home screen and it gets the Glowstage icon and its name, and on an iPhone it opens full screen like an app. The page also says when something holds your lights back, with a way out: the night limit (*Not tonight*), the automatic looks paused (*Resume*), or your on air look while you stream.
- A faster start: Glowstage loads about a sixth faster and holds less memory while it waits in the tray, and the genre AI lets go of its memory a minute after the light show stops.
- Easier with a keyboard and a screen reader: the color wheel, the stage editor (pick a light with the arrow keys and move it), the welcome tour and every dialog work with the keys, focus is always visible, switches and tabs say what they are, and faint text is easier to read. Bigger targets for small buttons, smooth scrolling stops when Windows is set to show less animation, the Light show page has headings a screen reader can jump between, and a countdown on Home (the focus timer) no longer takes the keyboard's place away.

**New in 0.9.8**

**Your lights, your way**
- Your own groups (Setup → Lights → *Your groups*): put lights together as *Desk*, *Bed*, *Living room*, up to 12 groups, and a light can be in more than one. Each group gets a card on Home with its own switch, brightness and *Color & effects*, and it's on the phone remote, in the tray, in Ctrl+K and in the links for Stream Deck and Home Assistant. Saving a preset can tick a group's lights in one click.
- Balance your lights (Setup → Lights → *Balance*): an LED strip's white is often bluish next to a bulb's, and one strip can outshine everything else. Per light, move *White balance* warmer or cooler and set what *Brightness at 100%* means for it, with *Show white* to hold it against your other lights. It applies to everything the light shows: colors, presets, effects, the light show, screen sync.
- Colors mixed up on a strip? (Setup → Lights → *Balance*): *Test colors* shows red, green and blue on that light, then answer what you saw ("when Glowstage shows red, this light shows green") and Glowstage sends that light its colors in the order its wiring expects. Many LED strips and Wi-Fi controllers are wired GRB or BRG.
- Choose how your lights change (Setup → Lights): at once, with a short glide (the default), or fading slowly over two seconds, for a preset, a color, switching on or off. Lights that can glide by themselves are told so (WLED, Hue, LIFX, Yeelight, Nanoleaf, Home Assistant), the ones inside your PC step through it. Dragging a slider still follows your hand at once.
- Your PC's lights glide into a new look: a preset, a color, switching on or off takes a third of a second instead of a jump, on the lights inside your PC (through OpenRGB), QMK keyboards and DMX fixtures. Dragging a slider or the color wheel still follows your hand at once, and Find a light still flashes.

**New looks**
- Three looks for the holidays (Color & effects → Effects, and on the phone): *Snowfall* lets soft flakes drift down through a winter-night blue, now and then one catching the light; *Holiday lights* is the classic string of red, green, gold and blue bulbs, each twinkling gently on its own; *Candy cane* moves soft red and white stripes along your lights (pick two other colors for a green and white one). All three run across your whole setup with *Flow*, and none of them flashes.
- Four more looks for any evening (Color & effects → Effects, and on the phone): *Fireflies* lets soft yellow-green glows rise and drift through a summer dusk; *Rain* runs thin drops down your lights; *Plasma* flows two colors through each other like an old screensaver; *Sunset* slides a slow orange, pink and violet glow along your setup with the sun sitting low. None of them flashes.
- Four calm looks for the evening (Color & effects → Effects): *Candle* flickers like a row of candles, with a gust now and then; *Ocean* rolls slow waves of deep blue and teal with a little foam; *Lava lamp* drifts warm blobs that merge and part; *Thunderstorm* lets dim clouds drift until lightning strikes, two or three quick flashes, sometimes a far one. The storm never flashes more than three times a second, holds a strike long enough for slow lights like Hue to show it, and with *Flow* on, a strike lights up one part of your setup most.
- PC load, a new look (Color & effects → Effects): your lights fill up like a bar as your PC gets busy, from your first color when it's idle to your second when it's working hard (green to red, say). The graphics card shows its own load, and with *Flow* on it's one bar across your whole setup. While it runs, Glowstage asks Windows twice a second how busy the processor and the graphics card are, and keeps nothing.
- Gradient, a new look (Color & effects → Effects): two colors blended along each light, through the colors between them on the color wheel, so red to blue passes a clear magenta instead of a dark purple. With *Flow* on, one gradient runs across your whole setup and every light takes the part where it stands.

**Colors from a picture**
- Light your room from a picture (Presets → *From a picture*): pick a photo and Glowstage takes its main colors and spreads them over your lights, left to right as they stand: a sunset over your desk, a forest in the corners. Strips and keyboards get a gradient between the colors around their place, bulbs one color each. Save it as a preset, or Undo puts your lights back. The picture is read on your PC and nothing is kept.
- Or take the photo with your phone: the phone remote has *Take or pick a photo*. Point it at a sunset, a poster or a plant and your lights take its colors right away, with *Save as preset* and *Undo* on the phone. The phone shrinks the photo before it sends it, so only a small copy goes over your Wi-Fi, and nothing is kept.
- Or use your wallpaper (*From a picture* → *Use my wallpaper*, or Ctrl+K): your lights take the colors of the picture on your desktop. With *Keep following my wallpaper* they change along when it does, for a slideshow or Windows Spotlight, until you change a light yourself.

**The light show**
- The light show in your own colors (Light show → *Colors* → *My colors*): pick 2 to 5 colors, your room's palette, your team's, a party theme, and the show keeps all of its moves (the beat, the build-ups, the drop, the chases across your setup) but uses only your colors, changing which leads from one phrase to the next. The big flash on a drop is white with *Strobe* on, otherwise the lightest of your colors. *Follow the music* is still the default.
- The light show can listen to a microphone, if you choose (Light show → *Listen to*): for music that doesn't come from your PC, a record player, a speaker on your phone, a party. It's off until you pick it, the sound is analyzed as it comes in and thrown away (nothing is recorded, saved or sent), the microphone is only open while the show or a music look runs, and it evens out a quiet room and a loud band by itself. *Start by itself* still only listens to what your PC plays, so talking in the room never starts the show.

**Games and streaming**
- Your room plays along with League of Legends (Setup → Games): your team's color, your health as a bar across your lights, a dim glow that fills back up while you wait to respawn, a gold flash for every kill (a bigger one for a multikill), a flash in the dragon's color when your team takes one, purple for the baron, and gold when you win. Never more than three flashes a second. It uses Riot's own Live Client Data API, which the game offers on your PC while you play: nothing reads or changes the game. Off until you switch it on.
- And with Dota 2 (Setup → Games → *Connect*): your side's color, the day and night of the map (brighter and warmer by day, dimmer and bluer at night), your health as a bar across your lights, grey while you wait to respawn, a gold flash for your kills, purple and gold for Roshan and the aegis, and gold when you win. It uses Valve's own game state integration, like for CS2: the game sends what happens, nothing reads or changes it. Add `-gamestateintegration` to Dota 2's launch options in Steam once, as Valve asks.
- And with Rocket League (Setup → Games → *Connect*, Steam or Epic): your team's color, your boost as a bar across your lights with a shimmer when you go supersonic, a sweep when your team scores (gold on top when it's your goal), a dim when the other team does, a flash for saves and demos, grey while your car is demolished, and your color held when you win. It uses Psyonix's own Stats API, which Connect switches on in the game's config; restart Rocket League once after connecting.
- On air with OBS Studio (Setup → Automatic → *When you stream or record in OBS*): while you stream or record (or only one of them), your lights take a preset you pick, and go back when you stop. Make a preset that only sets one light red and it's an ON AIR sign while the rest of the room stays as it is; a preset can also put your other lights on Screen sync for a game. It uses OBS's own WebSocket server (Tools → WebSocket Server Settings, OBS 28 or later), on this PC only; a password is kept encrypted for your Windows account and never shown again. It goes before every other automatic look.

**Timers and automatic looks**
- Focus timer (Home → *Focus timer*, or the tray): work for 15 to 50 minutes, then a 5 to 15 minute break, for up to 8 rounds. Your lights show your *Focus* preset (or stay as they are) while you work, and turn a calm soft green for the break, so you see from the corner of your eye that it's time to get up, without a sound or a pop-up. After the break they come back as they were, and after the last round they go back to how they were before you started. Lock the PC and walk away during a break and the timer keeps going.
- Presets in turn (Presets → *Take turns*): tick a few presets and Glowstage switches between them by itself, every 1 minute to 1 hour, in order or shuffled: an evening that moves from Cozy to Reading to Late night. Home shows which one is on and what comes next. It stops when you change a light yourself (locking your PC doesn't count), and an automatic look (a game, a film, a call) keeps the lights while it lasts.
- Night limit (Setup → Schedule): from a time in the evening until the morning, no light goes above the brightness you choose, whatever switches it on: a preset, a schedule, a game, the light show, your phone. A game that starts at 1 at night doesn't light the room up at full. The brightness you set isn't changed and comes back in the morning, a wake-up light still comes up to full, and Home has *Not tonight*.
- Pause the automatic looks for an hour, three hours or until tomorrow morning (Home, the tray, Ctrl+K, Setup → Automatic, or a link for Stream Deck): the look that's on ends and puts your lights back, and games, videos, calls, apps and music leave your lights alone until the pause is over. Home shows until when, with *Resume*.

**Control**
- Your own hotkeys (Setup → App → *Change keys*): give any action the keys you like, or none, and there are more actions: the PC's or the room's lights on or off, the next or previous preset, any preset by name, the focus timer, colors from your wallpaper, pausing the automatic looks. Macro keys that send F13 to F24 work without Ctrl or Alt. Keys Windows and other programs rely on (Ctrl+C, Alt+F4, Win+L and the like) are refused, and a key another program already has is named. Your old keys stay as they were until you change them.
- More from your phone: the phone remote sets a color (ten quick ones, or your phone's own color picker) or a look (Rainbow, Fire, Candle, Ocean, Thunderstorm, Screen sync and more) on every light that's on, and starts, skips or stops the focus timer. Stream Deck and other apps get links for a color and a look too.
- Use from other apps (Setup → Phone remote): Stream Deck (*Website* or an API request), AutoHotkey, Home Assistant and other apps can switch your lights on or off, pick a preset (by name, in any case), set the brightness, start the light show or the sleep timer, or undo, each with a link. Pick what it does, copy the link. It works while the phone remote is on, with the same secret key: *New link* shuts out every old one.
- Screen sync and Screen + sound follow the display you choose (Color & effects, with two or more displays): your main display, another one (named by where it stands, like *Right display · 2560 × 1440*), or *Where the game or video is*, which moves to a display only when something plays full screen there, and stays a few seconds after, so switching windows doesn't make your lights jump. A display you unplug is remembered for when it's back.

**Comfort**
- Soften flashes (Setup → App), for people who are sensitive to flashing light: no look flashes any more. Every light brightens and dims over a fraction of a second, so lightning, a kill in a game or the beat of the light show becomes a soft swell, and the light show leaves out its strobes. Every look already kept to three flashes a second at most; this takes the flashes out altogether.
- Skipped a version? The *What's new* card on Home shows what's new since the version you had, not only in the latest one.

**Fixed in 0.9.8**
- The window no longer freezes for a few seconds now and then: it looked for OpenRGB on your disk every second, and a disk that had gone to sleep (or a virus scanner busy with the folder) made it wait. It now looks once a minute, in the background.

**New in 0.9.7**
- Phone remote (Setup → Phone remote): scan the code with your phone and control your lights on the same Wi-Fi: all on or off, your PC's lights and your room's lights as two groups, every light on its own with its own switch and brightness, presets, the light show and the sleep timer, on a page made for thumbs. It's off until you switch it on, and it only answers to the code's link, which carries a secret key (*New link* shuts out the old one). Windows asks once whether Glowstage may use the network: allow it for private networks.
- Quick search: press Ctrl+K anywhere (or *Search* in the sidebar), type a few letters and press Enter to switch on a preset, open a light, put every light on an effect, jump to any part of Setup, or switch everything on or off, start the sleep timer or the light show, or undo.
- Wake up with a sunrise (Setup → Schedule → *Wake up slowly*): over 15, 30 or 45 minutes before the time you set, your room's lights come up from a dim deep red through amber to bright warm white, and stay on. Change a light meanwhile and it's yours again; *Stop* on Home puts the lights back as they were.
- Screen + sound, a new look for any game: your lights follow the screen, and every shot or explosion flashes them on the side it came from (a shot on your left lights the left of your setup; lights behind you flash along). It listens to the crack of a sound, so footsteps, engines, hi-hats and kick drums don't flash, and it never flashes more than three times a second. Pick it for full-screen games (Setup → Automatic) and it's there whenever you play.
- Typing ripples, a new look: every key you press lights up and sends a ripple across your keyboard (on keyboards whose LED layout Glowstage knows, like the MCHOSE GX87), and your other lights glow where the key sits, left to right. Speed sets how quickly it fades, the two colors the flash and the background. While it runs, Glowstage asks Windows 60 times a second which keys are down: it never sits between your keyboard and your programs, so typing and games feel the same. It keeps a key for three seconds as a spot on the board, and saves and sends nothing.
- A look per app (Setup → Automatic → Apps): pick an app that's open, choose its look (a preset, Screen sync or the light show), and your lights change while it's open and go back when you close it. *Focus* while Photoshop is open, *Gaming* while OBS is. It counts once the app has been open for a few seconds, so switching around doesn't flick the lights; the first app in the list goes first, before full-screen games and videos. Checking costs a fifth of a millisecond a second.
- On a call (Setup → Automatic): while an app uses your camera or microphone, your lights take the preset you pick, and go back when the call ends. It waits a few seconds, so a quick voice message doesn't flick them, and a game in full screen goes first, so voice chat while you play keeps your game's lighting. Glowstage only sees whether they're in use, never what they pick up.
- Backups (Setup → Backups): each day you use Glowstage, it keeps a copy of your presets, lights and settings, the last 14 days. Go back to one in two clicks, or save everything to a file and load it on another PC. Whatever you had before going back is kept as a copy too. And if the settings file is ever damaged, Glowstage starts from its newest copy and tells you, instead of starting with nothing.
- Sleep timer (Home, or the tray): the lights that are on fade out slowly over 15 minutes to an hour and a half, like a sunset, and switch off at the end. Next time they come on as they were; a light you change meanwhile is left alone, and cancelling puts the brightness back.
- Undo: picked a preset over the colors you'd just made, or switched everything off? *Undo* in the message that pops up puts your lights back as they were (also in the tray, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Z). Undo again to redo.
- Presets have their own menu (⋯ on each card): rename one (schedules, apps and automatic looks that use it follow the new name), update it with your lights as they are now, make a copy to change without losing the original, move it earlier, later or to the top (the tray, Home and hotkeys 1-9 follow that order), or delete it. Or drag a card onto another to give it that one's place. Saving over a preset keeps its place.
- Presets for some of your lights: when you save one, tick the lights it sets (all by default). Lights you leave out keep their look when you switch it on, so a room preset leaves your PC alone. Its menu says which lights it sets, and its card shows how many.
- Each group on Home ("In your PC", "In your room") has its own brightness slider, on/off button and *Color & effects*: dim the room and keep the PC bright, or give only the room a color. Switching a group off offers Undo.
- Whites by color temperature (Color & effects → Color): the *White* slider goes from 2200 K candlelight to 6500 K daylight in steps of 100 K, with its name ("2700 K · warm"), and works with the arrow keys. A *Recent* row keeps the last 8 colors you set by hand, across restarts. *Pick from screen* takes a color from anywhere on your screen: a photo, a game, your wallpaper.
- Schedules that follow the sun (Setup → Schedule): *At sunset*, *At sunrise*, on time or up to two hours before or after, for where you live. "30 min before sunset: Cozy" moves along with the seasons; each choice shows the time it means today.
- The tray icon shows your lights: their color when they're on, a rainbow for effects and the light show, grey when everything is off; its tooltip says how many are on.
- The tray menu has *Brightness* (100% to 10% for every light that is on) and *Lights* (the PC's lights or the room's lights on or off apart), each with Undo.
- Keys in the window: 1 to 9 switch on your first nine presets, L all lights on or off, S the light show, + and − brighter or darker, N the sleep timer, Z undo. Press ? (or Setup → App → Keyboard shortcuts) for the list, with the hotkeys that work from any program.
- More hotkeys (Setup → App → Hotkeys): Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Up and Down for brighter and darker (hold to keep going), +N for the sleep timer, +Z to undo.
- Flow (Color & effects → Effects): Rainbow, Spectrum, Breathing, Color wave, Comet and Aurora can run from light to light along the stage instead of each light on its own: one rainbow across your setup, a comet from the keyboard to the fans. Light show → Arrange sets where each light sits.
- The stage knows your desk: a mouse mat, mouse or headset stand (as OpenRGB names them) now stands in front of the monitor with the keyboard, not with the lights inside your PC on the right, so the light show and Flow run across it where it really is. Places you set yourself stay.
- Find a light (Setup → Lights): *Find* makes a light flash white for a few seconds, so you can see which name belongs to which light. Then it's back exactly as it was; nothing is saved.
- When lights stop answering, Home says which ones and why, with a tip for that kind of light (Wi-Fi, pairing, OpenRGB) and *Try again*. It waits a minute first, so a short reconnect doesn't show it.
- Report a problem (Setup → App): shows you a short summary first (your version, Windows, your lights and whether they answer, the last warnings from the log; never a key or a password), then opens a GitHub issue with it filled in, or copies it.
- After an update, Home says what's new in a short card, with a *Show me* for each new thing that takes you right to it.
- Easier without a mouse and with a screen reader: every control has a name (checked in the window's own accessibility tree), a window keeps the keyboard inside it and gives the focus back when it closes, and with Windows' *Show animations* off the effect previews hold still. In a narrower window, where the sidebar shows only icons, each keeps its name for a screen reader and shows it when you point at it.
- Fixed: after an update the window could stay empty. It was loaded through a small web server on a fixed port, and WebView2 kept an old page in its cache next to the new scripts. It now opens straight from its files.
- Fixed: with a Steam library on a hard disk that had spun down, the window could hang for seconds once a minute while Glowstage looked for CS2. It now looks in the background.
- A window call that takes more than 3 seconds writes where it waits into the log, so a slow window can be traced.
- The log now names an error a light reports without a message (like OpenRGB dropping its connection).

**New in 0.9.6**

**New in 0.9.5**

Cool and bright at noon, golden as the sun sets, warm in the evening, and from 23:00 a low amber glow for the night. Sunrise and sunset come from where you live (Windows' region setting), worked out on your PC: no location access, no internet. Use it as a preset, from the tray, or on a schedule.

**New in 0.9.4**
- Updates with one click: when a new version is out, Home says so with an *Update now* button. Glowstage downloads the new version from its GitHub release, checks it against the SHA-256 checksum GitHub lists for it, puts it in place of itself and restarts, in a few seconds. If anything can't be replaced (a virus scanner holding a file), it puts the old version back, so a failed update never leaves a broken app.
